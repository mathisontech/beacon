beacon · engine · the core · set 2026-09-12

Beacon is a hazard world model.

A general model of how hazards unfold and how we manage them: their triggers, cascades, and end signals, the force they give off and the force structures and people can take, what mitigation changes, and how every stage looks to a sensor. Applied to one place and its current signals, it becomes a running world state. Everything Beacon does is a use of that one object.

The modules
Hazard world modelThe general model: how things go down and how we handle them. A cause-and-effect engine that owns every model. Hazard models: triggers, event sequences, cascades, end signals; how the hazard itself unfolds. Impact models, the secondary layer: how much force the hazard gives off and, at the same time, how much force a structure can take, and whether a person can reach a shelter in time to call it one. Mitigation models: what interventions and technology change the outcome. The observation model: what each stage looks like to each sensor. It is the modeling-heavy, computational core, and it has no direct knowledge of any one person's context.
Learned fromEverything that went into building those models, sucked up into one system: world history, real event records, model knowledge, mitigation activity, technology change, outside data sources, and our own activity data. The world model is what all of that becomes.
World stateThe specific: this place, these people, now. Current signals and the place's priors (terrain, buildings, people, routes) with the world model applied to them. A weighted set of scenarios across three times: what happened, what is happening, what comes next. Not a single map. In Nepal, "earthquake" and "landslide" were both live for four days; a single-answer state would have picked wrong.
Expected signalsPredicted observations, rendered from the world state by the observation model, indexed by place, time, and sensor. Held in a cache because four modules consume the same object.
Perception engineNew data is compared against the expected signal for that place, time, and sensor. We expect a dry hill. The hill is wet. The residual is the output. Absence counts: three gauges expected to report and all silent is a large residual, which is exactly the Nepal failure nobody was watching for.
Inference engineTriggered by surprise. Given what is being seen, reweight the scenarios for what happened, what is happening, and what is likely next. Spawn new hypotheses from a library of hazard scenario templates, never freely. Roll the dynamics forward. Write the updated world state.
Planning engineOver the scenario distribution, weigh warning, guidance, and mitigation options: lives saved, timing, false-alarm cost. Produce a plan.
ActorExecutes the plan: alerts, guidance, tasking. Sends signals back down to phones, agencies, and responders.
Where the expected signals go

One object, four consumers. That is why it lives in a cache.

  1. Perception uses them as the comparator, including absence as a signal.
  2. Planning uses them as a sensing plan. Whichever expected signal would most separate the live hypotheses is the one worth tasking: ask phones inside a geofence for a photo, request a drone pass. This is active perception, and it is the mechanism behind viewshed and geofence corroboration.
  3. The actor and products use them as guidance content. "Water expected at Betrawati around 09:20" is an expected signal handed to a person. Pre-caching them to phones is what keeps guidance working when towers fall.
  4. Training uses expected versus realized as the calibration signal for the world model itself.

Two cautions for the build
  • Hypothesis explosion. Inference must draw from a library of hazard scenario templates. The three model libraries by world layer (geophysical, structure and infrastructure, living things) are that library. Without it, one surprising photo spawns unbounded explanations.
  • Cost lives in the world model, not in perception. Rendering expectations is what makes perception cheap enough to run at the edge, including on a phone with no signal. Design the cache so a phone can hold the expectations for its own area.
The Nepal morning, run through it

The seismic solver's "earthquake" is a residual against the expected waveform for a shallow event, which spawns the landslide hypothesis. The gauges going silent together is a residual against expected readings, which confirms a flood front. A photo of brown water at Betrawati localizes the front. Dynamics roll it forward to the school at plus fifty minutes. Planning picks "walk uphill now." The actor sends it with the place and the time in it.

How the product lines use it

Every product is a question asked of the world state, and a bridge that pays data back into it.

The product is not the chart above. The product is creating and reinforcing data bridges, then building the unified picture that makes those bridges worth crossing. Each product line asks the world state a different question on a different time axis, and each brings back data the picture did not have.

Household hardeningpremium households · before · could axisA paying household runs high-resolution scenario analysis on its own parcel: this fire, this flood, this wind, this shaking, against this construction and this lot. The planning engine ranks upgrades by how much they change the outcome. Brings back: parcel details, construction class, photos, home sensors, which sharpen the priors for the whole street.
Town, city, business hardeningofficials, businesses · before · could axisThe same analysis at scale. Burn-together and flood-together zones show that risk is shared at the neighborhood level even though we prepare as individuals, so the question becomes which shared fixes save the most per dollar and where. Brings back: infrastructure inventories, plans, budgets, and the mitigations actually built.
Emergency operations bridgeemergency services · during · is and will axesThe information bridge between services and the public in real time. Which roads are passable. Where units are and where they are needed. Which residents are already being helped adequately by neighbors, and who needs professional resources. That last question is answerable only because the public app is feeding the living-things layer of the world state. Brings back: unit positions, dispatch, tasking, and what each team could see at each minute.
Public and family appthe public · during · is and will axesWhat do I do now, which way is out, where are my people. Guidance and expected signals are cached to the phone before towers fall. People are sensors: a photo, a location, a status of need help or can help. Brings back: the ground truth the operations bridge runs on.
After-action reviewemergency investigators · after · was axisReplay the hazard with the reconstructed world state beside the picture each team actually had, minute by minute. Ask what the full picture would have changed and where the bridges failed. This is the four-track record from the posters, generated rather than reconstructed months later. Brings back: logs, radio, timelines, and the findings that become next year's hardening priors.
Research and APIscientists, partners · after · was axisThe world state and its replays offered as data. Models are validated against what actually happened and better ones come back into the libraries. Brings back: new models and verified datasets.

The bridges compound. What the public reports during feeds the operations picture. Operations logs feed the review. Review findings feed hardening priors. Hardened homes and towns change how the next event unfolds. Every product makes every other product's bridge more worth crossing.

Inside · the how-do-we-know engine

What is inside the hazard world model.

How do we know where somebody is? Whether someone needs help? Whether a building or a location is safe? Whether it is going to rain, and what the river will do? The world model is the answer to all of those questions, and it is the heart of the product. It is a stack of models, one rung per question. The data that exists today fills what it can. Where coverage runs out, a model fills the input. Each rung's answer is the next rung's input, so the stack models the whole world one layer at a time: what is here, what is happening to it, what event that makes, what it does to things and people, which way out, and what goes where. Applied to one place, now, the whole stack is the world state. The diagram after this one is the model in use.

The rungs, bottom to top
Data ingestion managerThe left rail. The data that exists today, by source, with a coverage map of what covers where, by place and by hour. Connectors and data contracts. Coverage varies everywhere, which is the reason the right rail exists.
Model researchThe right rail. The models that exist today: physics, published models, building codes, learned surrogates, event rules. The job is knowing which model fills which gap in the data, and how well. A river with no gauge still has a level; a hydrology model supplies it until a reading arrives.
Base map and object registryWhat is here. Terrain, buildings, roads, bridges, utilities, shelters, people. Known from elevation, footprints, road networks, and census; construction type and occupancy filled in from imagery. Every object above this rung is an entry in this registry, so a building's state has somewhere to live.
Condition modelsWhat is happening on the ground right now. Rain, river level, soil moisture, wind, ground motion, fire weather. Known from gauges, radar, satellites, and phones; where no gauge reports, weather and hydrology models fill the value, marked as filled.
Event models, one per hazardWhat event is unfolding and what comes next. Triggers, sequence, cascades, end signals; the footprint over time, where the force goes. Fed by conditions and observations; filled by physics, reduced models, surrogates, and event rules. These are the hazard models of the core.
Object state modelsIs this building, road, or bridge safe. The force the event gives off against the force each object can take, tracked as a state per object: intact, damaged, cut off, unknown. Known from photos, reports, and sensors on structures; filled by fragility curves per construction type. These are the impact models of the core.
People stateWhere is everyone and who needs help. Shared location, last seen, or inferred from home, work, and the hour; needs help or can help; whether shelter is in reach in time. Known from check-ins, shared locations, calls, and reports; filled by population and movement priors. The world model holds the models for this; the world state holds the people.
Evacuation and route modelsWhich way out, and can they make it in time. Which routes pass, given object states and the footprint; time to shelter for each person and group. When someone gets through a novel route, it is traced and shared with everyone behind them. Known from traffic and check-ins; filled by routing over the base map.
Mitigation and management modelsWhat goes where, and what changes the outcome. Resources, equipment capabilities (what a pump, a truck, a helicopter can do), who to send, where to stage. Known from unit positions, inventories, and what was done before; filled by a capability model per equipment type. These are the mitigation models of the core.
Event data aggregatorBeneath the stack. One record per event, every minute: everything known about it, what was seen, what was done. The stack reads it and writes to it. It has four views of the same record: what we see as model builders (everything, with the residuals); what emergency managers see (the operations picture, live); private, for a family (a person's location, shared with loved ones); and the public event page (what is happening, what to do).
How it is builtEach rung is fit on real events and replays from the knowledge base, validated by replaying the signals as they arrived, scored, signed, and pinned in the model registry. The observation model of the core is the "from" line of every rung: how that rung's state looks to each sensor. Training in the in-use diagram is this refit, running.

In use · the same diagram, grown

How the trained model is used: where each piece runs, and where its data lives.

The core sits at the center: the general world model on top, the specific world state beneath it. Around it: the stores, drawn as drums beside the module that writes them, and the two grey boxes from the core opened up into the product side. Sources come in at the bottom left and rise into perception. The actor's plan goes down the right into product backends, channels, and people. What people and clients send back crosses bridge intake and re-enters as a source. Colors are the domains that own each piece.

The four movements
  1. In. Sources and our own products arrive through ingestion: connect, land raw and immutable, curate under a data contract. They become the event store. Perception reads from the event store, never from a feed directly, so a replay and a live run look identical to the engines.
  2. Through. Perception writes residuals. Inference writes a new scenario set into world state history. The world state renders expected signals into the cache. Planning reads both and hands a plan to the actor, which writes what it said to records and audit. The engines start from a pinned version in the model registry, and the registry is fed by training over the knowledge base.
  3. Out. The actor hands alerts and tasking to the product backends, one per product. Each turns them into what its client needs: a push and a pack for a phone, a layer on the wall of an operations center, a job for a hardening run, a replay for a review. Channels carry them, including the liaison on the line. Phones hold a slice of the cache and a pack, so they keep working with no signal.
  4. Back. Photos, check-ins, reports, dispatch logs, and client data cross bridge intake, are validated, and enter ingestion as a source like any other. Outcomes, what was sent, received, acted on, and what actually happened, feed training. Training compares expected with realized and writes new signed versions to the registry. The loop closes in the lakehouse and in the registry, not inside a product.
The stores
Event storeEvery observation as it arrived, append-only, never edited. Only ingestion writes it. Perception, training, and replay read it. Named windows of it are the replay fixtures: Lahaina 2023, Nepal 2026. Replay and after-action review are only honest if this record cannot change.
Knowledge baseWhat the world model is learned from: world history, real event records, model literature, mitigation activity, technology change, outside sources, our own activity data. Training reads it. It is the general side's memory, as the event store is the specific side's.
Place priorsTerrain, buildings, roads, population for a place, versioned. Part of the world state, not the world model: the model is general, the priors are this place. Sharpened by what the hardening bridges bring back.
World state historyEvery scenario set inference wrote, timestamped. The past axis of the world state is this store's history. Planning, review, and training read it.
Expected-signals cacheAlready in the core. The hot store, by place, time, and sensor. Slices of it ship to phones so a phone can compare what it sees against what was expected with no signal.
Model registryWeights, surrogates, scenario template library versions, and the scorecard for each. Written by training with a human sign-off. Every engine starts from a pinned version. Nothing runs unpinned.
Product storesUsers and groups: accounts, households and parcels, families, neighborhoods, agencies, permissions. Client data brought in by onboarding: inventories, plans, dispatch. Product state: subscriptions, jobs, which pack each phone holds. Written by the product backends. Planning and the actor read who is where and who to reach. Legal owns the rules for this store.
OutcomesSent, received, opened, acted on, and what actually happened. Written by monitoring from the field. Read by training as the realized half of expected versus realized.
Records and auditWhat was said, when, to whom, under which model version. Written by the actor, immutable. The store an inquiry will ask for.
