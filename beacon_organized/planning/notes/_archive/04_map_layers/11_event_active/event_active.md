### 4.11 Event-Active Layers (Wildfire Example)

During a wildfire event, these layers become active:

- Population heatmap showing predicted and confirmed locations
- Risk heatmap (darkest red = most at risk due to fire/smoke direction + evacuation chokepoints, like Lahaina's Kuhua Camp with limited exits)
- Smoke prediction, heat, oxygen levels, all lethal exposure vectors
- **Oxygen levels layer** (real-time tracking during volcanic/wildfire events)
- **Visibility model layer** (for users and radio waves; contains sublayers)
- **Live weather layers** (real-time conditions overlaid during active events)
- Water locations + low water pressure reports
- 911 call locations + reported hazards/evacuation blockers
- Already-burned areas (black layer) including ground temperature estimation
- **"In the black" layer:** Predicted already stopped burning based on objects in area + satellite smoke readings
- Stuck people, injured people, confirmed casualties/fatalities (overheated phones)
- EMS drawing function for alerts to geofenced areas
- **Confirmed evacuation routes:** EMS-managed routes + routes users successfully used to get to safety
- Wave and water conditions for water evacuation feasibility
- Coast guard relevance if people evacuate into water
- Fire alarm detection layer (where alarms going off have been detected)
